What were your three best-paid years?

PERS bases your pension on your highest pay, averaged over three years. Use your gross yearly pay before taxes.

For OPSRP: the three years must be in a row.

What's in your IAP account?

Before you decide

  1. Ask PERS for an official estimate. Request one through your Online Member Services account or by calling PERS at 888-320-7377. Only PERS can confirm your numbers.
  2. Choose how your pension is paid. This estimate is for the option that pays only you. If you want a spouse or partner to keep receiving payments after you die, your monthly amount will be lower.
  3. Decide how to take your IAP. PERS can pay it as one lump sum, or in installments over 5, 10, 15 or 20 years. Ask a tax professional how each choice affects your taxes.
  4. Add Social Security to the picture. The Retirement Date Planner shows your pension, IAP and Social Security on one timeline and compares retirement dates.
  5. Plan for health insurance and taxes. The amounts here are before taxes and don't include health insurance costs.

      What this estimate leaves out

      Some Tier One and Tier Two members get more from a different method called Money Match, and PERS pays whichever method is higher. This estimate also doesn't include raises, cost-of-living increases, survivor options, split Tier One/OPSRP careers, or special rules for 911 telecommunicators, judges and legislators. For partial years before your full-benefit age, we estimate between PERS's yearly factors.
