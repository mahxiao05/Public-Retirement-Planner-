Questions and answers

Short, plain answers to the questions people ask most about retiring from a public job. Tap a question to open it. Each answer links to the full guide, where you'll find the official sources.

Most asked

How far ahead should I start planning to retire?

About three years out gives time to fix records, buy back service and compare dates. Even a few months ahead is enough to apply on time.

From Where to begin with retirement planning

Can I get Social Security and a public pension at the same time?

Yes. Since the Social Security Fairness Act, a government pension doesn't reduce your Social Security or a spousal or survivor benefit.

From Social Security explained

Can I change my mind after I start Social Security?

The start age generally sets your benefit for life, so treat it as a lasting choice. Social Security can tell you about any options for withdrawing an application in your own situation.

From When should I start Social Security?

What if I retire before 65?

You'll need health coverage until Medicare. Federal retirees can usually keep FEHB, and PERS retirees can buy a PERS retiree plan. Compare your choices.

From Where to begin with retirement planning

Do I have to sign up for Medicare at 65 if I'm retired?

Not by law, but if you have retiree coverage instead of coverage from a current job, delaying Part B usually brings a lifetime penalty and may leave your retiree plan unwilling to pay.

From When to sign up for Medicare

Does my spouse automatically get my pension if I die?

No. In most public plans your spouse gets a monthly pension only if you chose a survivor option at retirement. With single life, payments stop at your death, though some plans refund any unpaid contributions.

From Single-life or survivor pension: how to choose

How do I get an official pension estimate?

PERS members can use Online Member Services or ask PERS in writing. Federal employees ask their agency's benefits or HR office.

From Where to begin with retirement planning

Do I need a financial adviser?

Not to get your official numbers: your retirement system and Social Security provide them for free. Some people also pay an adviser for help with savings and taxes.

From Where to begin with retirement planning

Start here

Can I retire before I start Social Security?

Yes. Your pension and Social Security are separate. Many public employees retire first and start Social Security later.

From Where to begin with retirement planning

Is there a quick way to see if I can afford to retire?

Our Can I retire? tool gives a quick answer with Social Security at 62, 67 and 70. The planner goes into more detail.

From Where to begin with retirement planning

What is the Social Security COLA for 2027?

3.6%, starting with benefits paid in January 2027. SSI payments rise starting December 31, 2026.

From Retirement numbers for 2027

What is the FERS COLA for 2027?

Applying OPM's formula to the 3.6% CPI-W increase gives 2.6% for FERS and 3.6% for CSRS. Regular FERS retirees must be 62 to get it.

From Retirement numbers for 2027

What is the Social Security earnings limit for 2027?

$25,560 a year if you are under full retirement age all year, and $67,920 in the year you reach full retirement age.

From Retirement numbers for 2027

What is the 2027 TSP contribution limit?

Not announced yet. The IRS usually publishes it in the fall. The 2026 limit is $24,500, plus $8,000 catch-up at 50 and older.

From Retirement numbers for 2027

What is the 2027 Medicare Part B premium?

Not announced yet. CMS usually announces it in November. The 2026 standard premium is $202.90 a month.

From Retirement numbers for 2027

What is the maximum Social Security benefit in 2027?

$4,188 a month for someone retiring at full retirement age in 2027. Waiting until 70 can pay more.

From Retirement numbers for 2027

Oregon PERS

How is my Oregon PERS pension calculated?

Formula rate × years of service × final average salary. Tier One and Tier Two general service use 1.67%, OPSRP 1.5%. Tier One and Tier Two also get a Money Match calculation, and PERS pays the higher amount.

From Oregon PERS explained

What is the IAP?

The Individual Account Program is a savings account built from 6% of your pay since 2004. It's paid separately from your pension when you retire.

From Oregon PERS explained

How much is the PERS cost-of-living raise?

Up to 2% a year. Service after October 1, 2013 gets 1.25% on the first $60,000 of yearly pension and 0.15% above that.

From Oregon PERS explained

Can I retire from PERS and keep working?

Yes. Most PERS limits on working for PERS employers are lifted through 2034. Early retirees need a six-month break first to work unlimited hours.

From Oregon PERS explained

Does my PERS pension reduce my Social Security?

No. The Social Security Fairness Act ended those reductions for benefits from January 2024 on.

From Oregon PERS explained

How long does it take to get my first PERS payment?

PERS says within 92 days of your retirement date, back-dated to that date. The IAP is normally paid within 120 days.

From Oregon PERS explained

Who do I call at PERS?

Member Services at 888-320-7377, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays.

From Oregon PERS explained

Can I retire from PERS at 55?

Yes, if you're vested, but unless you have 30 years (Tier One or Tier Two) the pension is cut for life. At 55, Tier One keeps 77.5%, Tier Two 65.7%, and OPSRP general service under 30 years 44.0%.

From When can I retire from Oregon PERS?

Can a Tier One or Tier Two member retire before 55 with 30 years?

Yes. PERS lists 30 years of service as a full-benefit point for Tier One and Tier Two general service at any age.

From When can I retire from Oregon PERS?

What is the full retirement age for OPSRP?

65 for general service, or 58 with 30 years. For police and fire it's 55, or 53 with 25 years.

From When can I retire from Oregon PERS?

Is the early retirement cut permanent?

Yes. The reduced amount is your pension for life. Yearly cost-of-living raises are added to the reduced amount.

From When can I retire from Oregon PERS?

Can I take my IAP early and keep working?

Generally no. Since 2011 you retire from your pension and your IAP at the same time.

From When can I retire from Oregon PERS?

When will I get my first PERS check?

PERS says within 92 days of your retirement date, with payments back-dated to that date.

From When can I retire from Oregon PERS?

How do I know if I'm Tier One, Tier Two or OPSRP?

It depends on when you first started a PERS job: before 1996 is Tier One, 1996 to August 28, 2003 is Tier Two, and August 29, 2003 or later is OPSRP. Your PERS annual statement shows it.

From Tier One, Tier Two and OPSRP: what's the difference?

Is Tier One better than Tier Two?

Both use the same 1.67% formula. Tier One has an earlier full-benefit age (58 versus 60), a guaranteed rate on the member account, and for some, Formula Plus Annuity.

From Tier One, Tier Two and OPSRP: what's the difference?

Do OPSRP members get Money Match?

No. OPSRP pensions use the formula only. OPSRP members have no member account; their contributions go to the IAP.

From Tier One, Tier Two and OPSRP: what's the difference?

What is the Tier One assumed rate?

PERS credits Tier One regular member accounts with its assumed earnings rate, which PERS lists as 6.9%.

From Tier One, Tier Two and OPSRP: what's the difference?

Does a lump-sum vacation payout raise my pension?

Only for Tier One. Tier Two and OPSRP final average salaries leave it out.

From Tier One, Tier Two and OPSRP: what's the difference?

Is there a cap on the salary PERS counts?

Yes. For 2026 the limit is $245,724 a year, and it rises with inflation.

From Tier One, Tier Two and OPSRP: what's the difference?

Can I take my IAP and keep my pension for later?

No. Since 2011, PERS requires you to retire from the IAP and the pension at the same time.

From Oregon PERS IAP payout options

Can I change my IAP installment option later?

You can't switch to a different period or frequency, but you can ask for the remaining balance as a one-time lump sum at any time. That closes the account.

From Oregon PERS IAP payout options

Is the IAP guaranteed?

No. PERS says IAP accounts have no guaranteed rate of return. The balance can go down as well as up, before and after you retire.

From Oregon PERS IAP payout options

Can I roll my IAP into the Oregon Savings Growth Plan?

Yes, if you take a lump sum or 5-year installments. PERS transfers rollovers to OSGP automatically.

From Oregon PERS IAP payout options

How long does PERS take to pay the IAP?

PERS says the IAP is normally paid within 120 days of your retirement date.

From Oregon PERS IAP payout options

Do I pay the 10% penalty if I retire at 57?

The IRS exempts payments from a qualified plan if you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, but the exemption doesn't carry over to money you roll into an IRA. Confirm your situation with a tax professional.

From Oregon PERS IAP payout options

Who gets my IAP if I die?

The beneficiary you named for the IAP. If none is on file, PERS pays your surviving spouse, then your children in equal shares, then your estate.

From Oregon PERS IAP payout options

What is the default PERS option if I'm married?

For Tier One/Tier Two it's Option 3, and for OPSRP it's Half Survivorship, both with your spouse as beneficiary. Your spouse must consent to anything else.

From Oregon PERS survivor options

What happens to my pension if my beneficiary dies first?

Under Options 2 and 3 (or plain Full and Half Survivorship), nothing changes and payments stop at your death. Under 2A, 3A and the Increase options, you can ask to move up to the single life amount.

From Oregon PERS survivor options

Can I name my children as survivor beneficiaries?

Yes, a survivor option can name one living person, but if that person isn't your spouse and is more than 10 years younger, the survivor amount may be limited by IRS rules.

From Oregon PERS survivor options

Does OPSRP have a Refund Annuity?

No. OPSRP offers the Single Life Option and four survivorship options only.

From Oregon PERS survivor options

Can I change my PERS option after I retire?

Tier One/Tier Two members can usually change within 60 days after the first payment. OPSRP members can't change after their effective retirement date.

From Oregon PERS survivor options

Does my option choice affect my IAP?

No. The IAP has its own beneficiary designation and payout options.

From Oregon PERS survivor options

Does unused sick leave add years of service in Oregon PERS?

No. PERS values half of your allowed hours in dollars and adds that to your final average salary, not to your service credit.

From Unused sick leave and your PERS pension

Do OPSRP members get credit for unused sick leave?

No. Unused sick leave isn't part of the OPSRP pension calculation.

From Unused sick leave and your PERS pension

How many sick leave hours can count?

Up to 8 hours for each month worked, or your employer's lowest accrual rate if lower, minus hours you used. PERS then uses half of that total.

From Unused sick leave and your PERS pension

Does sick leave help if I get a Money Match pension?

No. Money Match is based on your member account, not your salary, so sick leave doesn't change it.

From Unused sick leave and your PERS pension

Can I get paid for sick leave and still have it count?

No. Hours paid out in cash are treated as used and aren't reported as unused sick leave.

From Unused sick leave and your PERS pension

What if I worked for more than one PERS employer?

Hours transferred from a former PERS employer can count if your new employer accepts them, under its policies or union contract.

From Unused sick leave and your PERS pension

Is the PERS 1,039-hour limit still in effect in 2026?

Only for early retirees who return to a PERS employer without a six-month break. Most other retirees have no PERS hour limit through December 31, 2034.

From Working after retiring from Oregon PERS

When does the SB 1049 work-after-retirement exemption end?

HB 2296 extended it through December 31, 2034. The standard limits return January 1, 2035 unless the Legislature changes the law.

From Working after retiring from Oregon PERS

Do I earn more PERS benefits if I go back to work?

No. Retirees working under these rules don't accrue new benefits, and no IAP contributions are made for them.

From Working after retiring from Oregon PERS

Can I work for a private company and keep my full PERS pension?

Yes. PERS doesn't limit hours or earnings for work with a private or non-PERS employer.

From Working after retiring from Oregon PERS

What is the OPSRP hour limit for retirees?

600 hours a calendar year. PERS treats 599.99 hours as the most you can work without becoming an active member again.

From Working after retiring from Oregon PERS

Does my PERS pension count toward the Social Security earnings limit?

No. Social Security counts only wages and self-employment income, not pensions.

From Working after retiring from Oregon PERS

Do Oregon PERS retirees get free health insurance?

No. PHIP is optional and retirees pay the premiums. Eligible Tier One/Tier Two retirees get a subsidy (RHIA or RHIPA) that lowers the cost.

From Oregon PERS retiree health insurance

How much is the RHIA subsidy?

$60 a month toward a PHIP Medicare plan, for Tier One/Tier Two retirees with 8 or more years of service who have Medicare Parts A and B.

From Oregon PERS retiree health insurance

Do OPSRP retirees get RHIA?

No. OPSRP retirees can join PHIP but aren't eligible for the RHIA or RHIPA subsidies.

From Oregon PERS retiree health insurance

How long do I have to sign up for PHIP after retiring?

90 days from your PERS effective retirement date.

From Oregon PERS retiree health insurance

Can I join PHIP later if I said no at retirement?

Only at another enrollment opportunity, such as becoming eligible for Medicare, losing 24 months of group coverage, or the open enrollment running October 1 to November 15, 2026.

From Oregon PERS retiree health insurance

Can my spouse stay on PHIP after I die?

Surviving spouses and dependents are eligible for PHIP. A surviving spouse can enroll within 90 days of the death or at another enrollment opportunity, and may qualify for a subsidy in some cases.

From Oregon PERS retiree health insurance

What is the 457(b) limit for 2026?

$24,500, plus $8,000 if you're 50 or older, or $11,250 instead if you turn 60 to 63 in 2026.

From Oregon Savings Growth Plan 457(b)

What is the 457(b) limit for 2027?

The IRS hadn't published it as of October 18, 2026. Check the IRS limits page once it's announced.

From Oregon Savings Growth Plan 457(b)

Can I use the special 457 catch-up and the age 50 catch-up together?

No. You can use only one in a given year, whichever allows the larger contribution.

From Oregon Savings Growth Plan 457(b)

Is there a penalty for taking money out of OSGP before 59½?

Not on your own 457(b) deferrals. The 10% additional tax applies only to money rolled in from another type of plan or an IRA.

From Oregon Savings Growth Plan 457(b)

Can I contribute to OSGP and a 403(b) at the same time?

Yes, if you have access to both. The 457(b) limit is separate from the 403(b) limit.

From Oregon Savings Growth Plan 457(b)

Does OSGP have a Roth option?

Yes. You can choose pre-tax, Roth, or both.

From Oregon Savings Growth Plan 457(b)

Does Oregon PERS accept a QDRO?

PERS divides benefits under a court order that uses its own award forms as exhibits, under Oregon law. An order written for a private plan without those forms may not be administrable.

From Oregon PERS and divorce

Can my ex-spouse get PERS benefits before I retire?

Under a Separate Account or Separate Benefit award, yes, once you reach earliest retirement eligibility. Under a Reduction or Deduction, payments start only when yours do.

From Oregon PERS and divorce

Is my IAP divided automatically with my pension?

No. The IAP needs its own award form. If the order doesn't mention the IAP, PERS treats it as no award.

From Oregon PERS and divorce

Can I remove my ex-spouse as my survivor beneficiary after I retire?

Only if you chose a pop-up option (2A, 3A or an OPSRP Increase option), or the order allows a beneficiary change and gives your former spouse an award.

From Oregon PERS and divorce

Who pays tax on my ex-spouse's share?

With a Reduction, each person is taxed on what they receive. With a Deduction, the member is taxed on the full payment.

From Oregon PERS and divorce

What happens if the order doesn't mention PERS?

PERS treats it as No Award with no restrictions.

From Oregon PERS and divorce

Washington DRS

How do I know if I'm in Plan 2 or Plan 3?

Log in to your DRS online account or check your annual statement. If you were hired in PERS, TRS or SERS and never made a choice, you were placed in the default plan for that time.

From Washington DRS explained

Is a Washington DRS pension taxed?

Generally yes. DRS says pension payments are generally taxable as federal income when you receive them (DRS: TRS Plan 2). Ask a tax professional about your own situation.

From Washington DRS explained

What happens to my contributions if I die before retiring?

They go to your beneficiary with interest. If you had at least 10 years in Plan 1 or Plan 2, or were vested in Plan 3, your spouse or partner may be able to take a monthly pension instead (DRS: PERS Plan 2).

From Washington DRS explained

Can I combine service from two DRS systems?

Often yes. DRS lets "dual members" count service in one system toward eligibility in another, but each pension is figured from its own system's service and rules. Ask DRS about your case.

From Washington DRS explained

Does more than 30 years help in Plan 1?

Not for the basic pension. Plan 1 caps it at 60% of AFC, which you reach at 30 years.

From Washington DRS explained

Who gives an official estimate?

DRS. Ask through your online account or call 800-547-6657.

From Washington DRS explained

Can I switch from Plan 2 to Plan 3?

No. DRS says your plan choice is permanent.

From DRS Plan 1, Plan 2 and Plan 3 compared

Is Plan 3 better than Plan 2?

Neither is better for everyone. Plan 2 pays a bigger guaranteed pension; Plan 3 trades half of it for an account that depends on markets and your contribution rate.

From DRS Plan 1, Plan 2 and Plan 3 compared

Can I change my Plan 3 contribution rate?

Only when you change to a different Plan 3 employer. A move within the same employer doesn't count.

From DRS Plan 1, Plan 2 and Plan 3 compared

What is the "2008 ERF"?

It is the early retirement factor for Plan 2 and 3 members with 30 years who were hired before May 1, 2013: 20% off at 55, shrinking to none at 62.

From DRS Plan 1, Plan 2 and Plan 3 compared

Do I have to be vested to keep my Plan 3 account?

No. DRS says vesting does not apply to the investment part of Plan 3. The 10-year (or 5-year) vesting rule is only for the pension part.

From DRS Plan 1, Plan 2 and Plan 3 compared

Why is my Plan 3 pension half of Plan 2?

Plan 3 uses a 1% multiplier because your own contributions go to the investment account instead of the pension.

From DRS Plan 1, Plan 2 and Plan 3 compared

What is the earliest age I can retire from PERS 2?

Age 55, if you have at least 20 years of service. The pension is reduced unless you are 65, or 62 with 30 years and hired before May 1, 2013.

From When can I retire from Washington DRS?

Can I retire at 62 with 30 years in Plan 2?

With a full pension, only if you were hired before May 1, 2013. Later hires with 30 years keep 85% at 62.

From When can I retire from Washington DRS?

Do partial years count?

Yes. Service credit is counted by the month, and the 5% ERF is prorated by month. DRS's full factor tables also go month by month.

From When can I retire from Washington DRS?

Does the early retirement cut end at 65?

No. The reduced pension is permanent. It does still get the yearly COLA in Plans 2 and 3.

From When can I retire from Washington DRS?

I'm in Plan 3 with 8 years at 56. Can I retire?

Not on the pension yet: early retirement needs 10 years. If you are vested, you can start the full pension at 65. Your investment account is available after you leave your job.

From When can I retire from Washington DRS?

Who confirms my exact date?

DRS. Call 800-547-6657 or use your online account.

From When can I retire from Washington DRS?

Can I change my survivor option after I retire?

Generally no. DRS says the option becomes final once retirement begins. Exceptions include the pop-up if your survivor dies first and a one-time chance to add a new spouse after marriage.

From Washington DRS survivor options

What happens if I divorce after choosing a survivor?

A court order filed with DRS can affect the survivor benefit. Ask DRS and your attorney before and after a divorce; DRS's rules on property orders are in WAC 415-02-500.

From Washington DRS survivor options

Does my survivor get the COLA?

For Plan 1, DRS says a survivor who receives your benefit keeps the optional COLA if you chose it. For Plans 2 and 3, ask DRS to show the survivor amounts with COLAs in your estimate.

From Washington DRS survivor options

Which option costs the least?

Option 3 (50%) has the smallest cut of the survivor options, and Option 2 (100%) the largest.

From Washington DRS survivor options

Is the Plan 3 investment account covered by these options?

No. The options apply to the Plan 3 pension. The investment account is separate, and DRS can explain how its beneficiary works.

From Washington DRS survivor options

Where can I see the exact factors?

On the DRS administrative factors page, or in your official estimate. Call DRS at 800-547-6657 with questions.

From Washington DRS survivor options

Federal FERS

How is the FERS annuity calculated?

High-3 × years of service × 1%, or 1.1% if you retire at 62 or older with at least 20 years. Special provisions employees get 1.7% for the first 20 years.

From FERS explained

What is the FERS COLA for 2027?

With Social Security's 3.6% raise, the FERS formula gives 2.6% for eligible FERS retirees. Most regular FERS retirees don't get raises until 62.

From FERS explained

Do I get the FERS supplement if I retire at 57 with 30 years?

Yes, if 57 is your MRA. The supplement runs until 62 and can be cut if you earn more than Social Security's limit.

From FERS explained

Can I keep FEHB in retirement?

Yes, if you retire on an immediate annuity and were enrolled for the 5 years before it starts, or since your first chance if that's shorter.

From FERS explained

How much does a survivor annuity cost?

A full one costs 10% of your annuity and pays your spouse 50% of the unreduced amount. A partial one costs 5% and pays 25%.

From FERS explained

How much do FERS employees pay toward the pension?

0.8% of pay if hired before 2013, 3.1% if hired in 2013, and 4.4% if hired in 2014 or later. The annuity formula is the same.

From FERS explained

Does my FERS pension reduce my Social Security?

No. FERS jobs pay Social Security tax, and the old reductions for public pensions have been repealed. Read more.

From FERS explained

What is the earliest I can retire under FERS?

For most employees, your Minimum Retirement Age (55 to 57) with at least 10 years. With fewer than 30 years, that annuity is reduced unless you wait to start it.

From When can I retire under FERS?

Can I retire at 57 with 20 years?

Yes, if 57 is your MRA, under MRA+10. Your annuity is cut 5% for each year under 62, but if you postpone the start to 60, the cut goes away because you have 20 years.

From When can I retire under FERS?

Do I get the FERS supplement under MRA+10?

No. The supplement only goes with unreduced retirements before 62: MRA with 30 years, 60 with 20 years, and special provisions.

From When can I retire under FERS?

Does unused sick leave help me reach 30 years?

No. Sick leave adds to the service used in the annuity formula, but it doesn't count toward the years you need to be eligible.

From When can I retire under FERS?

Is it worth working until 62?

With 20 or more years, retiring at 62 or later raises your multiplier from 1% to 1.1%, a 10% bigger annuity for life. Whether that beats two extra years of payments depends on your health, savings and plans.

From When can I retire under FERS?

Do I lose FEHB if I postpone my MRA+10 annuity?

Your enrollment stops when you leave, but you can get it back when your annuity starts if you had FEHB for the 5 years before you left.

From When can I retire under FERS?

Is the FERS military buyback worth it?

For most people the deposit is small next to the extra annuity it buys, because each year adds 1% of your high-3 every year for life. It can be less clear if you must waive a military pension to get the credit.

From FERS military buyback

Can I pay the military deposit after I retire?

No. Under FERS the military deposit must be paid in full to your agency before you separate. Unpaid post-1956 military service gives no FERS credit.

From FERS military buyback

When does interest start on a FERS military deposit?

For people first hired under FERS on or after January 1, 1987, interest starts two years after your first FERS employment date. After that, a variable rate is charged and compounded each year (4.25% for 2026).

From FERS military buyback

Does buying back military time change my MRA?

No, your MRA depends only on your birth year. The extra years can let you retire unreduced at your MRA with 30 years, or at 60 with 20, instead of taking a reduced MRA+10 annuity.

From FERS military buyback

Do I need to waive military retired pay?

Usually, if you want the same years counted for FERS. Exceptions include combat-related disability retired pay and reserve retired pay under Chapter 1223 of Title 10.

From FERS military buyback

What forms do I need for the military deposit?

Your DD 214, form RI 20-97 to get your military earnings, and SF 3108 (or your agency's version) to apply. Your agency computes the bill on OPM Form 1514.

From FERS military buyback

Does military service count for the FERS supplement?

Paid-up military years can help you reach an MRA+30 or 60+20 retirement, which qualifies you for the supplement. Ask your benefits office whether the military years also change the supplement amount, which is figured from your federal earnings.

From FERS military buyback

Does FERS pay out unused sick leave?

No. There is no cash payment. It is added to your service when OPM computes your annuity, at 100% for retirements on or after January 1, 2014.

From FERS unused sick leave credit

How many sick leave hours equal a month of FERS service?

About 174 hours. OPM uses a 2,087-hour year, so 2,087 hours is one full year of credit.

From FERS unused sick leave credit

Can sick leave help me reach 30 years for an MRA+30 retirement?

No. Sick leave is not used to establish eligibility to retire. It only increases the annuity once you qualify.

From FERS unused sick leave credit

Does sick leave count for a deferred FERS annuity?

The credit applies to people who separate with title to an immediate annuity or die in service, so a deferred annuity generally gets no sick leave credit.

From FERS unused sick leave credit

Does sick leave raise my high-3?

No. Your high-3 is based on basic pay actually received. Sick leave only adds to the service years in the formula.

From FERS unused sick leave credit

What happens to leftover days?

After sick leave is added to your service, any days that don't make a full month are dropped from the annuity computation.

From FERS unused sick leave credit

Can I get a FERS pension if I leave before retirement?

Yes, with at least 5 years of creditable civilian service. The annuity starts later, at 62 with 5 years, 60 with 20, your MRA with 30, or a reduced amount at your MRA with 10.

From Leaving federal service early: deferred or postponed

What is the difference between deferred and postponed FERS retirement?

Both pay a delayed annuity. A postponed MRA+10 annuity, available with 10 or more years, lets you reenroll in FEHB and FEGLI when it starts if you met the five-year rule. A deferred annuity does not.

From Leaving federal service early: deferred or postponed

Do I keep FEHB if I leave federal service before retirement?

Not while you're gone. You can buy up to 18 months of Temporary Continuation of Coverage at full cost plus 2%. You may reenroll when a postponed MRA+10 annuity begins, if you qualify.

From Leaving federal service early: deferred or postponed

Should I take a refund of my FERS contributions?

It depends on your years and plans. A refund returns only your own contributions and ends your annuity rights for that service unless you return and repay it.

From Leaving federal service early: deferred or postponed

Does a deferred FERS annuity get the supplement or COLAs?

It gets no FERS supplement. Like other regular FERS annuities, COLAs don't apply before age 62.

From Leaving federal service early: deferred or postponed

How is part-time service counted for FERS?

It counts fully toward eligibility. The annuity is computed at full-time pay and then multiplied by the share of full-time hours you actually worked.

Yes. File Form W-4V to have 7%, 10%, 12% or 22% of each payment withheld.

What is the 457(b) limit for 2026?

$24,500, plus an $8,000 catch-up at 50 or older, or $11,250 at ages 60 to 63 if your plan offers it.

From 457(b) deferred compensation plans explained

Can I contribute to a 457(b) and a 403(b) in the same year?

Yes. The 457(b) limit is separate, so you can contribute the full amount to each plan.

From 457(b) deferred compensation plans explained

Can I use the special 457 catch-up and the age 50 catch-up together?

No. In any year you use one or the other, whichever allows more.

From 457(b) deferred compensation plans explained

Is there a penalty for withdrawing from a 457(b) before 59½?

No 10% additional tax applies to governmental 457(b) withdrawals, except on money rolled in from another type of plan or an IRA. Regular income tax still applies to pre-tax money.

From 457(b) deferred compensation plans explained

Do I have to make Roth catch-up contributions in 2026?

Only if your prior-year Social Security wages from the plan's employer were more than $150,000. Employees not covered by Social Security are not subject to the rule.

From 457(b) deferred compensation plans explained

Should I roll my 457(b) into an IRA when I retire?

It depends. An IRA may offer more investment choices, but you lose the 457's penalty-free access before 59½. Compare fees and choices before deciding.
