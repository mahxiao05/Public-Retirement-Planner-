Washington DRS retirement calculator

Updated October 31, 2026

Short answer

A Washington DRS pension is 2% × years of service × average final compensation in Plans 1 and 2, and 1% in Plan 3, which adds an investment account. Plans 2 and 3 pay in full at 65 and can start from 55 with a cut. Enter your numbers to see your monthly pension.

Your numbers

For PERS, TRS and SERS members. Your plan, service and average final compensation are in your DRS online account.

Plans 2 and 3: your highest-paid 60 months in a row. Plan 1: your best 24 months (PERS) or two fiscal years (TRS).
Matters only if you retire early with 30 or more years.

    What this calculator leaves out

    • Plan 3's investment account. It depends on what you put in and how it grows. The full planner adds it.
    • Survivor options. Options 2, 3 and 4 pay you less so a survivor keeps a share. See DRS survivor options.
    • Raises after you retire. Plans 2 and 3 get a yearly raise of up to 3% based on inflation.
    • LEOFF, WSPRS, PSERS and judges. These plans have their own formulas and ages. Ask DRS.
    See a worked example

    Sam is in PERS Plan 2, started in 2008, and has an average final compensation of $80,000.

    • At 65 with 25 years: 2% × 25 × $6,667 a month = about $3,333 a month, with no cut.
    • At 60 with 20 years: 2% × 20 × $6,667 = $2,667 before the cut. Retiring 5 years early, DRS's actuarial factor leaves about 62% of that, about $1,663 a month.
    • At 60 with 30 years: Sam started before May 1, 2013, so the cut is only 5% at 60. That gives about $3,800 a month.

    Common questions

    When can I retire from Washington DRS?

    Plans 2 and 3 pay a full pension at 65 with 5 years. You can start from 55 with 20 years in Plan 2 or 10 years in Plan 3, with a cut. Plan 1 pays in full at any age with 30 years, at 55 with 25, or at 60 with 5.

    How big is the early retirement cut?

    With fewer than 30 years, DRS uses actuarial factors: about 9% off for one year early, and much more the earlier you go. With 30 or more years, the cut is 5% a year if you started on or after May 1, 2013, or smaller still (none at 62) if you started before.

    What is average final compensation?

    In Plans 2 and 3, your average pay over your highest-paid 60 months in a row. In PERS Plan 1 it is your best 24 months, and in TRS Plan 1 your two highest-paid fiscal years in a row.

    Does more than 30 years help in Plan 1?

    Not for the basic pension. Plan 1 caps it at 60% of average final compensation, which you reach at 30 years.

    Who gives an official estimate?

    DRS. Ask through your online account or call 800-547-6657.
