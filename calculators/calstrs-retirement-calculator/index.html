CalSTRS retirement calculator

Updated October 31, 2026

Short answer

A CalSTRS pension is service credit × age factor × final compensation. Educators hired before 2013 are usually on 2% at 60, and those hired since on 2% at 62. Both top out at 2.4% per year of service. Enter your numbers to see your monthly pension.

Your numbers

For CalSTRS Defined Benefit members. Your formula and service credit are in your myCalSTRS account.

Your highest average yearly pay over 36 months in a row (12 months for some 2% at 60 members with 25 or more years).

    What this calculator leaves out

    • The longevity bonus. Members with 30 years by the end of 2010 get $200 to $400 a month more.
    • Beneficiary options. They lower your payment so a beneficiary keeps a share. Your CalSTRS estimate shows the amounts.
    • Retiring before 55. 2% at 60 members with 30 years can start at 50 at lower factors. Ask CalSTRS.
    • The yearly raise. A simple 2% of your starting benefit is added each September after your first year.
    See a worked example

    Jordan is on 2% at 60 with final compensation of $95,000.

    • At 60 with 25 years: 25 × 2.0% × $95,000 = $47,500 a year, about $3,958 a month.
    • At 62 with 30 years: the age factor is 2.27%, plus the 0.2% career factor for 30 years, capped at 2.4%. 30 × 2.4% × $95,000 = $68,400 a year, or $5,700 a month.

    Common questions

    What is the earliest I can retire from CalSTRS?

    Age 55 with at least five years of service credit. Members on 2% at 60 with at least 30 years can retire at 50.

    What is the career factor?

    If you're on 2% at 60 and retire with at least 30 years of earned service, CalSTRS adds 0.2% to your age factor, up to a total of 2.4%.

    Will I get Social Security as a California teacher?

    Not from teaching, because CalSTRS jobs don't pay Social Security tax. You may get it from other work or as a spouse, and since 2025 WEP and GPO no longer reduce it.

    Can I change my option after I retire?

    Within 30 days of your first payment, yes. After that, generally only after events like a death, divorce or marriage.

    Who gives an official estimate?

    CalSTRS. Use the calculator in your myCalSTRS account, or ask for a written estimate.
