Single life or survivor option: which pays more?

Updated October 24, 2026

Short answer

A survivor option pays you less each month so your spouse keeps getting part of your pension after you die. Whether it comes out ahead depends on who lives longer, and by how much. Enter the amounts from your retirement system's estimate to see the trade-off in dollars.

Your numbers

Use the monthly amounts from your official estimate. Oregon PERS, Washington DRS, CalPERS and OPM all show each option.

For a 100% option, the same as the line above. For 50%, half of it.

If you die atTotal paid, single lifeTotal paid, survivor optionBetter choice

Totals add up every monthly payment to you and then to your spouse, in today's dollars, without raises or taxes.

What else to weigh

  • The choice is usually permanent. Most systems let you change only in a short window after your first payment, or if your spouse dies first and the plan has a "pop-up" feature.
  • Health insurance can depend on it. Under FERS, a spouse generally needs a survivor annuity to keep FEHB coverage after you die. Check your system's retiree health rules.
  • Social Security changes too. When one spouse dies, the household keeps only the larger of the two Social Security benefits. See survivor benefits.
  • Life insurance instead? Some people take the single-life pension and buy life insurance for their spouse. It only works if the policy stays affordable and in force for as long as needed. Our guide on single life or survivor pension explains the risks.

Plan-specific guides: Oregon PERS options · Washington DRS options · FERS survivor annuity.

See a worked example

Chris is 62 and can take $3,000 a month for life, or $2,700 a month with a survivor option that keeps paying a spouse $2,700. The spouse is 60.

  • The cost: $300 a month, or $3,600 a year, while Chris is alive.
  • The payoff: if the spouse lives to 90, the survivor option pays the household more in total whenever Chris dies before about age 88.

Change the spouse's age or how long they might live to see where the line moves.

Common questions

Can I change my choice later?

Usually not. Most systems lock the choice once payments start. Some allow a change in a short window after the first payment, or raise your pension back up if your spouse dies first.

Is a survivor option a good deal if my spouse is younger?

It costs more, because the pension is likely to be paid for longer. It also protects more years of your spouse's life, so it can still be worth it.

Does my spouse have to agree to single life?

In many systems, yes. Washington DRS, FERS and others need your spouse's signed consent before you can choose an option with no survivor benefit.

What about life insurance instead?

Some people take the larger single-life pension and buy life insurance for their spouse. It only works if the policy stays affordable and in force as long as needed.

An illustration, not advice. Nobody knows how long they'll live. Talk with your spouse, and ask your retirement system to explain each option before you choose.
