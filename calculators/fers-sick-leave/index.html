How much does my unused sick leave add to my FERS annuity?

Rules checked October 18, 2026, against opm.gov

Short answer

FERS counts 100% of your unused sick leave as extra service when it figures your annuity. OPM converts hours using a 2,087-hour work year, so about 174 hours equals one month. Sick leave raises the amount, but it can't help you reach a retirement age or service requirement.

Your numbers

On your last leave and earnings statement.

How we worked this out

    The rule

    Since January 1, 2014, FERS retirees get credit for all of their unused sick leave in the annuity computation (5 U.S.C. 8415). OPM adds the sick leave to your actual service, then drops any extra days that don't make a full month. A month is 1/12 of a 2,087-hour year, about 173.9 hours, and a day is 2,087 ÷ 360, about 5.8 hours. Read OPM's explanation on its FERS computation page.

    • It adds to the amount only. Sick leave can't make you eligible sooner. If you need 30 years for an unreduced annuity at your Minimum Retirement Age, sick leave doesn't count toward the 30.
    • It doesn't count toward the FERS supplement. Whether it can help you reach the 20 years needed for the 1.1% multiplier at 62 isn't spelled out on OPM's pages, so ask your benefits office before you count on it.
    • Part-time schedules convert differently. Ask your benefits office if you worked part time.
    • Leave you're paid for doesn't count. Annual leave is paid out as a lump sum, not added to service.

    Our full guide covers the details: FERS sick leave credit explained. To see your whole annuity, try the FERS and TSP Planner.

    See a worked example

    Pat has 1,200 hours of unused sick leave, 25 years, 4 months and 10 days of service, and a high-3 of $95,000.

    • Sick leave as time: 1,200 hours is 6 months and 26 days.
    • Added to service: 25 years, 4 months and 10 days becomes 25 years and 11 months. Leftover days are dropped.
    • What it adds: 7 extra months × 1% × $95,000 ÷ 12 = about $554 a year, or $46 a month, for life.

    Common questions

    Does sick leave help me retire sooner?

    No. It only raises the amount of your annuity. It can't help you reach your Minimum Retirement Age, the years of service you need, or the 30 years for an unreduced MRA+10 annuity.

    Does sick leave raise the FERS supplement?

    No. The supplement is figured from your actual FERS service only.

    Should I use up my sick leave before I retire?

    Each hour you keep adds a little to your annuity for life, so many people save it. Using it for real illness is still what it's for. Your agency can tell you your exact balance.

    Does annual leave count the same way?

    No. Unused annual leave is paid to you as a lump sum when you retire. It isn't added to your service.

    An estimate, not advice. We aren't part of OPM. Your agency's benefits office can confirm your sick leave balance and service.
