Is the FERS military buyback worth it?

Rules checked October 18, 2026, against opm.gov

Short answer

To count active-duty military service toward your FERS annuity, you pay a deposit of 3% of your military basic pay (3.25% for 1999 and 3.4% for 2000). Interest starts 2 years after you begin FERS-covered work, and you must pay in full before you leave federal service. Because each year adds 1% of your high-3 for life, the deposit often pays for itself in a few years.

Your numbers

Your branch of service sends an estimate of your military basic pay when you request one through your agency (OPM's form RI 20-97).

How we worked this out

    About interest

    If you were first hired on or after January 1, 1987, the deposit is interest free for 2 years from the date you first started work under FERS. After that, interest is charged at a variable yearly rate set by the Treasury and compounded once a year (CSRS/FERS Handbook, chapter 23). This calculator shows the deposit without interest. Your payroll office will tell you the exact balance, including any interest, and you can pay in installments through payroll deduction.

    Why it matters

    • It counts toward eligibility too. Paid-for military time counts toward the years you need to retire, which can move your retirement date earlier and can remove the MRA+10 reduction.
    • Pay before you leave. OPM says the deposit must be paid before you separate from federal service. Unpaid military time doesn't count for FERS (OPM: Service credit).
    • Military retirees: if you get military retired pay, you usually must waive it to count the same service toward FERS, with exceptions for some disability and Reserve retirements (OPM: Military retired pay).

    Our full guide walks through the forms and steps: FERS military buyback explained. To see your whole annuity, use the FERS retirement calculator.

    See a worked example

    Jordan served 4 years on active duty after 2000 and earned $96,000 in military basic pay over those years. Jordan expects a high-3 of $90,000 and will retire under the regular 1% multiplier.

    • Deposit: 3% of $96,000 = $2,880, plus any interest.
    • What it adds: 4 years × 1% × $90,000 = $3,600 a year, or $300 a month, for life.
    • Payback: $2,880 ÷ $300 = under 10 months of retirement.

    Even with a few years of interest added, a deposit like this usually pays for itself within the first year or two of retirement.

    Common questions

    How do I find out how much military pay I earned?

    Ask your agency's human resources office to help you request an estimate of earnings from your branch of service with OPM form RI 20-97. The deposit is figured from that estimate.

    Can I pay the deposit in installments?

    Yes. Most agencies let you pay through payroll deduction over time. Interest keeps building on any unpaid balance once the 2-year interest-free period ends.

    What happens if I retire without paying?

    For FERS, unpaid military service generally doesn't count at all: not toward the amount and not toward the years you need to retire. OPM says the deposit must be paid before you leave federal service.

    I get military retired pay. Can I still buy back my time?

    Usually you must waive your military retired pay to count the same service toward FERS. There are exceptions for some disability and Reserve retirements, so check with your benefits office before deciding.

    An estimate, not advice. We aren't part of OPM or the military. Your agency's payroll office sets the exact deposit and interest.
