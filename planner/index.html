When can I retire, and what will I live on?

This planner puts your pension, your retirement savings account and your Social Security on one timeline, and can include your spouse. Answer a few short questions, then compare different retirement dates side by side. It takes about five minutes.

This is an estimate, not advice. We aren't part of any government agency. Only your retirement system and Social Security can tell you your actual benefits, so ask both for official estimates before you set a date. Rules checked October 24, 2026.

Question 1

When were you born?

Your birth date sets your age today, the ages when you can retire, and your full retirement age for Social Security.

Which retirement system are you in?

Each system has its own rules. We'll ask questions that fit yours.

Not covered yet: Washington LEOFF, PSERS and WSPRS, and CalPERS safety formulas. Their rules are different.

When did you start working in a PERS job?

Your start date puts you in one of three PERS groups. Each one has its own formula.

What kind of job do you have?

How many years have you worked in PERS jobs?

PERS calls this your service credit. Each year adds to your pension. We'll add the years you keep working until the date you pick.

Half years are fine, like 24.5.

Where to find it: your yearly PERS member statement lists your service. You can also see it in your PERS Online Member Services account.

What were your three best-paid years?

PERS bases your pension on your highest pay, averaged over three years. Use your gross yearly pay, before taxes.

For OPSRP: the three years must be in a row.

What's in your IAP account?

The Individual Account Program (IAP) is a separate savings account PERS keeps for you. Since 2004, part of each paycheck has gone into it. If you don't know, leave it at 0.

It's on your yearly IAP statement.
Before taxes.
5% is a middle-of-the-road guess. Real returns go up and down.

What's in your PERS member account?

Tier One and Tier Two members have a member account from contributions made before 2004. PERS also figures your pension a second way from this account, called Money Match, and pays you whichever way gives more.

On your yearly PERS member statement. Leave at 0 if you don't know, and we'll skip Money Match.

This is not your IAP. Your statement lists the member account separately. Don't include voluntary or IAP money.

Which DRS plan are you in?

Washington's PERS, TRS and SERS each have Plans 1, 2 and 3. The formulas are the same across the three systems, so you only need to pick your plan.

When did you start in your DRS plan?

How many years of DRS service do you have?

DRS calls this your service credit. Each year adds to your pension. We'll add the years you keep working until the date you pick.

Half years are fine, like 22.5.

Where to find it: your DRS online account and yearly statement show your service credit.

What's your average yearly pay in your best 5 years in a row?

DRS calls this your average final compensation. Use gross pay, before taxes. For most people it's their last few years.

A yearly amount, before taxes.

What's in your Plan 3 investment account?

Plan 3 members put 5% to 15% of each paycheck into their own investment account. If you don't know, leave the balance at 0.

On your DRS or DCP account online.
Before taxes.
Plan 3 lets you choose 5% to 15%.
5% is a middle-of-the-road guess. Real returns go up and down.

Which CalPERS formula are you on?

Your formula is printed on your CalPERS Annual Member Statement and in myCalPERS. Most people who first joined in 2013 or later are on 2% at 62.

When were you first hired as a California educator?

CalSTRS has two formulas. Your Retirement Progress Report in myCalSTRS shows yours.

What kind of PERSI member are you?

Police officers and firefighters have a higher percentage and earlier ages.

How many years of service do you have?

Each year adds to your pension. We'll add the years you keep working until the date you pick.

Half years are fine, like 22.5.

Where to find it: your yearly statement or online account shows your service credit.

What's your average yearly pay in your best years?

Use gross pay, before taxes. For most people it's their last few years.

A yearly amount, before taxes.

Do you have other retirement savings?

For example a 457(b), 403(b), the PERSI Choice 401(k), or CalSTRS's Defined Benefit Supplement. If you don't have any, leave the balance at 0.

What kind of federal job do you have?

Some jobs retire under special rules with earlier retirement ages and a larger formula.

How many years of federal service do you have?

Count your creditable civilian service under FERS. We'll add the years you keep working until the date you pick.

Half years are fine.

Where to find it: your service computation date and sick leave balance are on your Leave and Earnings Statement.

What's your highest average yearly pay over three years in a row?

FERS calls this your "high-3". Use basic pay, including locality pay, but not overtime or bonuses. For most people it's their last three years.

A yearly amount, before taxes.

What's in your TSP?

The Thrift Savings Plan (TSP) is your federal retirement savings account. Your agency adds money too. If you don't know, leave the balance at 0.

On tsp.gov or your quarterly statement.
Basic pay, before taxes.
5% gets you the full agency match.
5% is a middle-of-the-road guess. Real returns go up and down.

What do you know about your Social Security?

Your Social Security statement has the most accurate number. If you don't have it handy, we can estimate from your pay.

On the statement it's the amount "at full retirement age", usually 66 or 67.

Get your statement: sign in at ssa.gov/myaccount (my Social Security). It's free.

Think of what a typical year of your career would pay today.
Count years with pay from any job.

Does Social Security tax come out of your paycheck now?

Are you married?

A spouse can get Social Security based on your work, and you can choose a pension that keeps paying your spouse after you die.

From their Social Security statement. Enter 0 if they won't get any on their own work.

How should your pension be paid?

At what age are you thinking of retiring?

This is when you'd stop working and start your pension. You can compare other dates on the results page.

When would you start Social Security?

You can start as early as 62 or wait until 70. Each month you wait raises your payment for life. Your retirement date and your Social Security date don't have to be the same.
