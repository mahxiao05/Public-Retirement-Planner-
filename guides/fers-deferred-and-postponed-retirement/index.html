  3. Leaving federal service early: deferred or postponed

What happens to my FERS pension if I leave federal service early?

Rules checked October 18, 2026, against opm.gov

Short answer

With at least 5 years of civilian service, you keep a right to a FERS annuity later: unreduced at 62 with 5 years, 60 with 20 or your MRA with 30, or reduced at your MRA with 10. If you leave with 10 or more years and had health and life insurance for the 5 years before you left, you can usually get FEHB and FEGLI back when a postponed MRA+10 annuity starts. If you take a refund of your contributions instead, you give up the annuity for that service.

Your three choices when you leave

If you leave a FERS job before you can retire on an immediate annuity, OPM lists two paths for your basic benefit: take a refund of your retirement contributions, or, with at least five years of creditable service, wait and draw a monthly annuity later (OPM: Former employees). If you have 10 or more years, the "later" option splits into two flavors that matter a lot for insurance.

ChoiceWhat you get
Deferred annuityMonthly annuity later, no FEHB or FEGLI
Postponed MRA+10 annuityMonthly annuity later, FEHB and FEGLI if you qualify
RefundYour contributions now, no annuity for that service

Your Thrift Savings Plan account is separate. It stays yours whichever choice you make (see TSP withdrawal options).

Deferred retirement

OPM says former FERS employees "may be eligible for a deferred annuity at age 62 or the Minimum Retirement Age" (OPM: Types of retirement). You need at least 5 years of creditable civilian service, and the annuity can start at:

Age and serviceAnnuity
62 with 5 yearsUnreduced
60 with 20 yearsUnreduced
MRA with 30 yearsUnreduced
MRA with 10 to 29 yearsCut 5% a year under 62

Source: OPM: FERS eligibility. Your MRA is 55 to 57 depending on birth year. The annuity uses the normal formula: 1% of your high-3 for each year of service. The high-3 is based on the pay you earned before you left.

A deferred annuity has real limits:

  • No FERS supplement. OPM says you are not eligible for the supplement "if you receive a deferred benefit."
  • No sick leave credit. Credit for unused sick leave applies to people who leave with an immediate annuity (sick leave guide).
  • No insurance. OPM says deferred annuitants are "not eligible to reenroll in any health benefits, life insurance coverage, or dental and vision benefits."
  • You must apply. Payments don't start on their own. You file form RI 92-19 about 60 days before you want benefits to begin (OPM: RI 92-19 instructions).

Postponed MRA+10 retirement

If you have at least 10 years of service, including 5 civilian, you can use the MRA+10 rule after you leave. You pick a start date from your MRA onward. Starting at your MRA means a cut of 5/12 of 1% for each month you are under 62 (5% a year). OPM explains that "the longer you wait (up to 2 days before your 62nd birthday), the smaller the reduction." If you have 20 years and start at 60 or later, there's no cut at all (OPM: Postponing an MRA+10 annuity).

This is often called postponed retirement. The annuity amount is figured the same way as a deferred annuity. The big difference is insurance, below. OPM's FAQ also notes that if you die before filing, your family's rights are protected because you are treated as a retiree.

Health and life insurance

To carry the Federal Employees Health Benefits program (FEHB) into retirement, OPM generally requires that you were covered for the five years right before you retire, or for all of your service since your first chance to enroll (OPM: Keeping health insurance after retirement). Federal life insurance (FEGLI) has a similar five-year rule.

  • Postponed MRA+10: coverage stops when you leave. When the annuity starts, OPM's RI 92-19 instructions say you may reenroll in FEHB and FEGLI if you were enrolled for the 5 years of service just before separation, or continuously since your first opportunity. OPM pays the government share of the premium again.
  • Deferred: no return to FEHB, FEGLI or FEDVIP.
  • In the gap: you can keep FEHB for up to 18 months under Temporary Continuation of Coverage (TCC), but you pay the full premium plus a 2% charge. Contact your former agency within 60 days of leaving.

Because the rules hinge on how OPM classifies your application, confirm in writing how your case will be handled before you resign. For coverage options in the gap years, see Health insurance before 65 and Keeping FEHB and FEGLI in retirement.

Taking a refund, and what you lose

You can ask OPM to return your FERS contributions with form SF 3106. Here's what the form's instructions and OPM's former-employee page say (OPM: SF 3106 instructions):

  • You get only your own contributions. That's the 0.8%, 3.1% or 4.4% of pay you paid in, not your agency's much larger share.
  • Interest is paid only if your service totals more than one year, at the rate on government securities. The contributions aren't taxable, but the interest is, and OPM withholds 20% of the interest unless you roll it over.
  • You give up the annuity for that service. The form warns that a refund "will permanently eliminate your retirement rights" for the refunded service, unless you later return to federal service and repay it.
  • Repaying later. OPM says people covered by FERS on or after October 28, 2009 may redeposit refunded FERS service if they come back. If you don't repay, the service still counts toward eligibility but not toward the annuity amount.
  • Your spouse is notified. Current and former spouses must each complete SF 3106A to document notice. A refund also ends any former spouse's FEHB eligibility based on your service.
  • Timing limits. No refund until 31 days after separation, and not within 31 days of the earliest date you could start any annuity.

For someone with only a few years, a refund can be reasonable. For someone with 10 or more years, it often means trading a lifetime annuity, and possibly retiree health coverage, for a small check.

Part-time service

Part-time years count fully for eligibility. OPM's handbook says "part-time service is creditable to the same extent as full-time service" when deciding whether you can retire (OPM: Handbook, Chapter 55).

The annuity amount is prorated. OPM computes the annuity as if you had worked full time, using the full-time rate of pay for your high-3, then multiplies it by a proration factor: the hours you actually worked divided by the hours you would have worked full time (2,087 hours a year for service after March 1, 1986), rounded to the nearest percent.

Example

Riley is a made-up FERS employee born in 1972 (MRA 57). Riley leaves at 47 with 15 years of service: 10 full time and 5 half time. Riley's high-3 at the full-time rate is $80,000 and Riley had FEHB and FEGLI for the last 5 years.

  1. Proration factor: 10 full-time years plus 5 years at half time is 12.5 years of hours out of 15, or 83%.
  2. Full annuity before proration: $80,000 × 1% × 15 = $12,000 a year.
  3. Prorated: $12,000 × 83% = $9,960 a year.
  4. Start at MRA (57): 5 years under 62, cut 25%, so about $7,470 a year. FEHB and FEGLI can resume.
  5. Start at 62: no cut, about $9,960 a year. If OPM processes it as a postponed MRA+10 annuity, FEHB and FEGLI can resume then too. Riley should confirm this before leaving.
  6. Refund instead: Riley would get back only Riley's own contributions plus interest, and no annuity.

Riley's annuity would not get COLAs before 62. See FERS COLAs.

What to do next

  1. Count your years. Know whether you have 5, 10, 20 or 30 years, and how much was part time.
  2. Check your insurance history. Confirm five continuous years of FEHB and FEGLI before your last day.
  3. Get it in writing. Ask HR for an estimate of a deferred or postponed annuity and confirmation of how insurance will work.
  4. Think twice about a refund. Read the SF 3106 warnings before you sign.
  5. Apply later. File RI 92-19 about 60 days before you want the annuity to begin.

Common questions

Can I get a FERS pension if I leave before retirement?

Yes, with at least 5 years of creditable civilian service. The annuity starts later, at 62 with 5 years, 60 with 20, your MRA with 30, or a reduced amount at your MRA with 10.

What is the difference between deferred and postponed FERS retirement?

Both pay a delayed annuity. A postponed MRA+10 annuity, available with 10 or more years, lets you reenroll in FEHB and FEGLI when it starts if you met the five-year rule. A deferred annuity does not.

Do I keep FEHB if I leave federal service before retirement?

Not while you're gone. You can buy up to 18 months of Temporary Continuation of Coverage at full cost plus 2%. You may reenroll when a postponed MRA+10 annuity begins, if you qualify.

Should I take a refund of my FERS contributions?

It depends on your years and plans. A refund returns only your own contributions and ends your annuity rights for that service unless you return and repay it.

Does a deferred FERS annuity get the supplement or COLAs?

It gets no FERS supplement. Like other regular FERS annuities, COLAs don't apply before age 62.

How is part-time service counted for FERS?

It counts fully toward eligibility. The annuity is computed at full-time pay and then multiplied by the share of full-time hours you actually worked.

General information, not advice. We aren't part of OPM. Ask your agency's HR office for a written estimate before you resign, and contact OPM about a deferred or postponed annuity after you leave.
