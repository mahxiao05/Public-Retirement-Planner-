When can I retire under FERS?

Rules checked October 18, 2026, against opm.gov and the U.S. Code

Short answer

With no reduction, you can retire at your Minimum Retirement Age with 30 years, at 60 with 20 years, or at 62 with 5 years. Your Minimum Retirement Age is between 55 and 57, depending on the year you were born. With 10 to 29 years you can also go at your Minimum Retirement Age, but the annuity is cut 5% for each year you're under 62 unless you delay its start.

The four ways to retire

These are the "immediate" retirements, where your annuity starts within 30 days of leaving (OPM: FERS eligibility):

Age and serviceWhat you get
MRA + 30 yearsFull annuity, plus the FERS supplement until 62
60 + 20 yearsFull annuity, plus the supplement until 62
62 + 5 yearsFull annuity; 1.1% multiplier with 20 or more years
MRA + 10 yearsReduced annuity, no supplement

Your annuity is your high-3 average salary times your years of service times 1%, or 1.1% if you retire at 62 or older with at least 20 years (OPM: computation). Your years of service can include military time you've bought back and, for the formula only, unused sick leave. See the FERS military buyback and how sick leave adds to your annuity.

Minimum Retirement Age by birth year

Born before 1948: 55. Born 1953 to 1964: 56. Born 1970 or later: 57. In between, it goes up by 2 months for each birth year. For example, 1967 is 56 and 6 months (5 U.S.C. 8412(h)). See the full table.

Your MRA is only the earliest age you can leave with an immediate annuity. It doesn't by itself mean a full annuity. For that you need 30 years at your MRA, or one of the other age and service pairs above.

How the MRA+10 cut works

If you reach your MRA with at least 10 but fewer than 30 years, you can retire right away under what's called MRA+10. OPM reduces the annuity by 5/12 of 1% for each full month you're under 62, which is 5% a year. The cut doesn't apply if you have 30 years, or if you have 20 years and your annuity starts at 60 or later (OPM: computation).

You can shrink the cut by postponing the start of your annuity. You leave your job, then pick a start date any time from your MRA to just before 62. The later the start, the smaller the cut (OPM: types of retirement). Things to know about postponing:

  • No supplement. MRA+10 retirees never get the FERS supplement, whether they start right away or later.
  • Health insurance pauses. Your FEHB enrollment ends when you leave. You can keep it for up to 18 months through Temporary Continuation of Coverage at full cost plus 2%, then re-enroll when your annuity starts, as long as you had FEHB for the 5 years before you left (OPM). See FEHB and FEGLI in retirement.
  • Raises start at 62. Cost-of-living adjustments still begin at 62. See FERS cost-of-living raises.

Police, firefighters and air traffic controllers

Law enforcement officers, firefighters and some similar jobs can retire at 50 with 20 years of covered service, or at any age with 25 years (5 U.S.C. 8412(d)). Air traffic controllers have similar rules. Most law enforcement officers and firefighters must leave at 57, or when they reach 20 years if they're older than 57 by then. Air traffic controllers must leave at 56 on the same terms (5 U.S.C. 8425). These retirements use a richer formula and get cost-of-living raises right away. Read more in FERS special provisions.

Why 62 matters

  • The 1.1% multiplier starts at 62 if you have 20 years. That's a 10% raise in your annuity for life.
  • The MRA+10 reduction ends at 62.
  • The FERS supplement stops at 62.
  • Regular FERS retirees start getting cost-of-living increases at 62.
  • Social Security can start at 62, though it's smaller than at your full retirement age.

Example: 60 or 62?

Example

Jordan is a made-up federal employee born in mid-1967, with 22 years of service in mid-2026 and a $95,000 high-3. Jordan's MRA is 56 and 6 months.

  1. Retire at 60 (mid-2027): 23 years, full annuity at 1%. $95,000 × 1% × 23 = $21,850 a year, plus the FERS supplement until 62.
  2. Retire at 62 (mid-2029): 25 years at 1.1%. $95,000 × 1.1% × 25 = $26,125 a year, about 20% more for life.
  3. The difference: $4,275 a year. Retiring at 60 gives two extra years of annuity and supplement, but each later year pays less.
  4. What if Jordan left now, at 59? With 22 years, that's an MRA+10 retirement. Starting the annuity right away would cut it by 5% for each of the 3 years under 62, or 15%. If Jordan postponed the start to 60, the cut would go away, because Jordan has at least 20 years. Either way, there'd be no supplement.

These figures leave out raises in pay, any survivor annuity and taxes. Your agency's estimate will be more exact.

If you leave before you're eligible

If you leave federal service before you qualify for any immediate annuity, and you have at least 5 years of civilian service and don't take a refund of your contributions, you can get a deferred annuity later: at 62 with 5 years, at your MRA with 30 years, or at your MRA with 10 years (reduced the same 5% a year under 62). A deferred annuitant can't re-enroll in FEHB or FEGLI and doesn't get the supplement (OPM: types of retirement). That loss of health coverage is one reason the gap between "eligible" and "not quite eligible" matters so much.

There's also an early retirement for people pushed out by a reorganization or a reduction in force: 50 with 20 years, or any age with 25 years (OPM: FERS eligibility).

What to do next

  1. Find your MRA. Look up your birth year in the table above.
  2. Confirm your service. Ask HR for your service computation date and check for any unpaid military deposit or gaps.
  3. Get an estimate for two dates. Ask your agency's benefits office for estimates at your earliest date and at the first date with no reduction.
  4. Check health coverage. Make sure you'll have FEHB for the 5 years before you retire.
  5. Compare the numbers. Use the FERS planner or the quick Can I retire? tool.

Common questions

What is the earliest I can retire under FERS?

For most employees, your Minimum Retirement Age (55 to 57) with at least 10 years. With fewer than 30 years, that annuity is reduced unless you wait to start it.

Can I retire at 57 with 20 years?

Yes, if 57 is your MRA, under MRA+10. Your annuity is cut 5% for each year under 62, but if you postpone the start to 60, the cut goes away because you have 20 years.

Do I get the FERS supplement under MRA+10?

No. The supplement only goes with unreduced retirements before 62: MRA with 30 years, 60 with 20 years, and special provisions.

Does unused sick leave help me reach 30 years?

No. Sick leave adds to the service used in the annuity formula, but it doesn't count toward the years you need to be eligible.

Is it worth working until 62?

With 20 or more years, retiring at 62 or later raises your multiplier from 1% to 1.1%, a 10% bigger annuity for life. Whether that beats two extra years of payments depends on your health, savings and plans.

Do I lose FEHB if I postpone my MRA+10 annuity?

Your enrollment stops when you leave, but you can get it back when your annuity starts if you had FEHB for the 5 years before you left.
