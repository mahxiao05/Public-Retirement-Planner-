Oregon PERS

Which PERS pension option should protect my spouse?

The Oregon PERS payment options at retirement, from Option 1 to the joint and survivor choices, what each one costs you, and why the decision is close to permanent.

Rules checked October 18, 2026, against oregon.gov/pers

Short answer

A single life option (Option 1, or the Single Life Option in OPSRP) pays the most each month but stops when you die. Survivor options pay your beneficiary 100% or 50% of your payment for life, in exchange for a smaller payment to you. If you're married, your spouse must sign off on anything else, or PERS uses a 50% survivor option by default. Tier One/Tier Two members can change only within 60 days after the first payment; OPSRP members can't change after their retirement date.

How the options work

When you apply to retire, PERS figures your pension, then asks how you want it paid. Every option is worth about the same on average over everyone's lifetimes. The difference is who gets paid and for how long. An option that keeps paying someone after you die has to pay you less each month to balance out the extra years of payments.

The option affects only your pension. Your IAP account has its own beneficiary and its own payout choices.

Tier One and Tier Two options

Tier One and Tier Two members choose from 13 options. Most are the seven below; the rest are "lump-sum" versions that pay out your member account in cash and turn only the employer-funded part into a monthly payment (PERS: Tier One/Tier Two preretirement guide, June 2026).

OptionAfter you die
Option 1Nothing is paid. Highest monthly amount.
Refund AnnuityBeneficiary gets what's left of your member account in a lump sum
15-year CertainIf you die before 180 payments, the rest go to your beneficiary
Option 2Beneficiary gets 100% of your payment for life
Option 2ALike Option 2, with a "pop-up" to Option 1
Option 3Beneficiary gets 50% of your payment for life
Option 3ALike Option 3, with a "pop-up" to Option 1

The Refund Annuity

The Refund Annuity pays you for life. If you die before your member account (your own pre-2004 contributions plus earnings) has been paid back through your payments, your beneficiary gets the remainder in a lump sum. PERS notes the account is "typically exhausted within nine to eleven years," so after roughly a decade there's usually nothing left to refund.

The pop-up options (2A and 3A)

Under Option 2 or 3, if your beneficiary dies first, your payment doesn't change, and it stops when you die. Options 2A and 3A add a safety valve: if your beneficiary dies before you, or you divorce a spouse beneficiary after retiring, you can ask in writing to switch to the higher Option 1 amount. PERS calls this moving to Option 1; planners often call it a pop-up. The pop-up only happens if you send PERS a written request (form 778 after a death, form 777 after a divorce).

Total lump sum

Tier One and Tier Two members can also take a Total (double) Lump-sum: the member account plus a matching amount from the employer account, with no monthly pension afterward. If your Option 1 payment would be under $200 a month, PERS pays a one-time lump sum instead.

OPSRP options

OPSRP has five options with plain-English names (PERS: OPSRP preretirement guide, June 2026):

OPSRP optionSimilar Tier One/Two option
Single Life OptionOption 1
Full SurvivorshipOption 2
Half SurvivorshipOption 3
Full Survivorship IncreaseOption 2A
Half Survivorship IncreaseOption 3A

OPSRP has no Refund Annuity, no 15-year Certain and no lump-sum options. The "Increase" options move you to the Single Life amount if your beneficiary dies first, or if you divorce a spouse beneficiary after retiring, once you send proof and a signed request. If your OPSRP pension would be under $200 a month before reductions, PERS pays it as a one-time lump sum.

What each option costs you

PERS sets the cost of each option with actuarial factors that depend on your age and, for survivor options, your beneficiary's age. The factors are published in PERS's actuarial equivalency factor tables, effective January 1, 2026. We don't quote survivor percentages here because they vary widely by age, but the pattern is consistent:

  • 100% costs more than 50%. Full survivorship (Option 2) cuts your payment more than half survivorship (Option 3), because the survivor's payment is twice as large.
  • A younger beneficiary costs more. The longer your beneficiary is expected to live after you, the bigger the reduction to your payment.
  • The pop-up costs a little extra. PERS says the Increase options pay "somewhat lower benefits" than the plain Full and Half Survivorship options.
  • The Refund Annuity and 15-year Certain cost a little. For the part of a Tier One/Two pension bought from a member account at age 65, the January 2026 tables give $7.37 a month per $1,000 under Option 1, $7.11 under the Refund Annuity and $7.07 under 15-year Certain. That's roughly 3.5% to 4% less for the guarantee.
Example

Jordan, a made-up Tier Two member, is 62 and married to Sam, who is 58. Jordan's Option 1 estimate is $3,000 a month. The reductions below are made up to show how the choice works; Jordan's real figures come from a PERS estimate.

  1. Option 1: Jordan gets $3,000. Sam gets nothing from the pension after Jordan dies.
  2. Option 3: Jordan gets a reduced amount, say $2,800. After Jordan dies, Sam gets half, $1,400, for life.
  3. Option 2: Jordan gets a lower amount, say $2,600. Sam keeps getting $2,600 for life.
  4. Options 2A or 3A: a bit less than Options 2 or 3, but Jordan's payment rises to the Option 1 amount if Sam dies first.

Cost-of-living raises apply to all of these amounts each year.

Who can be your beneficiary

  • Survivor options (2, 2A, 3, 3A and the OPSRP survivorship options): one living person, with proof of age. It can't be an estate, trust or charity.
  • Refund Annuity and 15-year Certain: you can name an estate or trust, and you can change the beneficiary at any time.
  • A non-spouse much younger than you: if your beneficiary isn't your spouse and is more than 10 years younger, IRS rules limit how much the survivor can get, so the payment may be reduced.
  • Married members: under the spousal consent rules, your spouse (or registered domestic partner in OPSRP) must consent to any other option or beneficiary, often with a notarized signature. Without consent, Tier One/Two members get Option 3 and OPSRP members get Half Survivorship, both naming the spouse.

Can you change your mind?

Treat the choice as permanent. The windows to change are short:

  • Tier One and Tier Two: changes are generally allowed only within 60 days after your first payment. The change is retroactive to your retirement date, and you repay any overpayment. You also can't change the beneficiary on Options 2 or 3 after that window.
  • OPSRP: options and beneficiaries can't be changed after your effective retirement date. Before that date, you can send a new application. After it, the only route is to move your retirement date later, and PERS must get the new application before your first payment is issued.

After those windows, the main ways your option changes are the pop-up (2A, 3A and the Increase options) and certain court orders after a divorce. See how PERS handles divorce.

What to do next

  1. Get an official estimate for each option with your beneficiary's birth date, through Online Member Services or by calling PERS at 888-320-7377.
  2. Compare against other ways to protect a spouse, such as Social Security survivor benefits and life insurance. Our Social Security timing guide explains how your start age affects a surviving spouse.
  3. Plan the spousal consent if you choose anything other than the default; it may need a notary.
  4. Read your plan's guide: the Tier One/Tier Two or OPSRP preretirement guide.

Common questions

What is the default PERS option if I'm married?

For Tier One/Tier Two it's Option 3, and for OPSRP it's Half Survivorship, both with your spouse as beneficiary. Your spouse must consent to anything else.

What happens to my pension if my beneficiary dies first?

Under Options 2 and 3 (or plain Full and Half Survivorship), nothing changes and payments stop at your death. Under 2A, 3A and the Increase options, you can ask to move up to the single life amount.

Can I name my children as survivor beneficiaries?

Yes, a survivor option can name one living person, but if that person isn't your spouse and is more than 10 years younger, the survivor amount may be limited by IRS rules.

Does OPSRP have a Refund Annuity?

No. OPSRP offers the Single Life Option and four survivorship options only.

Can I change my PERS option after I retire?

Tier One/Tier Two members can usually change within 60 days after the first payment. OPSRP members can't change after their effective retirement date.

Does my option choice affect my IAP?

No. The IAP has its own beneficiary designation and payout options.
