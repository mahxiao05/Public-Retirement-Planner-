Does my government pension lower my Social Security?

Rules checked October 18, 2026, against ssa.gov and irs.gov · Reviewed by Max Ma

Short answer

No, not anymore. The Social Security Fairness Act, signed on January 5, 2025, ended the two rules that used to cut Social Security for many people with public pensions. They don't apply to benefits for January 2024 and later. If you held off applying because of those rules, you may need to apply now, since back pay is usually limited to 6 months.

What changed

Two rules used to reduce Social Security for people who also got a pension from a job that didn't pay Social Security tax:

  • The Windfall Elimination Provision (WEP) could lower your own Social Security retirement benefit.
  • The Government Pension Offset (GPO) could lower or wipe out a spouse's or widow(er)'s benefit.

The Social Security Fairness Act ended both. It was signed into law on January 5, 2025, and Social Security says the two rules no longer apply to benefits payable for January 2024 and later. Social Security says the rules had affected more than 2.8 million people (SSA: Social Security Fairness Act).

For people who were already getting a reduced check, Social Security did the work on its own. It began adjusting payments on February 25, 2025, and most people saw the new monthly amount starting in April 2025. Each person also got a one-time back payment covering the increase back to January 2024. Social Security says that by July 7, 2025 it had sent more than 3.1 million of these payments, totaling $17 billion. Some increases were small, and some were more than $1,000 a month.

What it means for you

  • Your Social Security is figured the same way as everyone else's, from your 35 highest years of earnings that paid Social Security tax. The figure that formula produces is called your primary insurance amount.
  • If your public job didn't pay Social Security tax, those years still don't count toward your Social Security. They count toward your pension instead. The law didn't add credit for those years. It only stopped the extra cut.
  • A spouse or widow(er) benefit is no longer reduced by your own government pension.
  • You still need 40 credits, about 10 years of work, to qualify for your own retirement benefit (SSA: credits). You can earn up to 4 credits a year. In 2026 you get one credit for each $1,890 of covered earnings, and in 2027 for each $1,970 (SSA: 2027 fact sheet).
  • All the normal rules still apply, including the cut for starting before your full retirement age and the earnings test if you work while collecting early.

Many public employees were never affected

If your public job paid Social Security tax, as most Oregon PERS jobs and all FERS jobs do, WEP and GPO usually didn't apply to you in the first place. Check your pay stub for "Social Security" or "OASDI". Your pension and your Social Security were always figured separately and paid in full.

The people the old rules hit hardest worked part of their career in a job that didn't pay Social Security tax. That includes many teachers and some police officers and firefighters in certain states, and federal workers under the older CSRS system. If that describes you or your spouse, this law is the one that changed your numbers.

An example with a spouse

Example

Rosa is a made-up retired teacher. Her school district didn't pay Social Security tax, so she gets a teacher pension and has too few credits for her own Social Security. Her husband, Dan, spent his career in a private job that did pay it. His full-retirement-age amount is $2,600 a month, and he has already started his benefit.

  1. Spousal benefit: a spouse can get up to half of the worker's full-retirement-age amount. Half of $2,600 is $1,300 a month if Rosa starts it at her own full retirement age (SSA: benefits for a spouse).
  2. Before the law: the Government Pension Offset subtracted an amount tied to her teacher pension, which in a case like Rosa's could wipe out the spousal benefit completely.
  3. Now: no offset. Rosa can get the full $1,300 a month on top of her pension.
  4. If Dan dies first: as a widow at full retirement age or older, she could get up to 100% of Dan's benefit instead of the spousal amount (SSA: if you are the survivor).

If Rosa starts the spousal benefit before her full retirement age, it's smaller. At 62 it can be as little as 32.5% of Dan's full amount.

Read more in our guides to spousal benefits and survivor benefits.

If you never applied

Many people didn't bother applying because they knew WEP or GPO would shrink or erase the benefit. Social Security says you may need to file an application now, and the date you apply can affect when benefits begin. The law did not change the usual back-pay limit: for retirement and survivor benefits, back pay is generally limited to 6 months before the month you apply (SSA: Social Security Fairness Act).

  • Your own or a spousal benefit: apply online at ssa.gov. For a spousal benefit, Social Security says to choose "Family Benefits" so you're considered for everything you qualify for. You can also call 1-800-772-1213.
  • A widow or widower benefit: this can't be done online. Call 1-800-772-1213, weekdays from 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time.
  • Already getting a reduced check: if Social Security has your address and bank details, you don't need to do anything.

Social Security also warns that it will never ask you to pay to start, raise or release your benefits.

What can still lower your check

A government pension no longer cuts your Social Security. A few other things still can:

RuleWhat it does
Starting earlyStarting at 62 pays 70% of your full amount for life if you were born in 1960 or later.
Earnings testWorking before full retirement age holds back $1 for every $2 you earn over $25,560 in 2027.
Medicare premiumsPart B premiums are usually taken out of your check once you're on Medicare.
Income taxPart of your benefit can be taxed if your income is high enough.

Sources: SSA: retirement age and benefit reduction and SSA: earnings limits. A pension isn't counted as earnings for the earnings test, but it does count toward the income that decides whether your benefits are taxed. Social Security says you may owe federal tax on benefits if your combined income is over $25,000 filing alone or $32,000 filing jointly (SSA: income taxes and your benefit). See are Social Security benefits taxed? and the earnings test explained.

Check your record

Your Social Security statement at ssa.gov/myaccount shows your earnings history and your estimated benefit at 62, your full retirement age and 70. Look at each year. Years from a job that didn't pay Social Security tax will show as zero, which is expected. A missing year from a job that did pay it is an error worth fixing while you still have your W-2s.

What to do next

  1. Read your statement. Sign in at ssa.gov/myaccount and note your estimates at 62, full retirement age and 70.
  2. Check your spouse's record too. If one of you had a job without Social Security, the spousal and survivor numbers may now matter a lot.
  3. Apply if you held off. If you're past your full retirement age and never applied because of WEP or GPO, apply soon, since back pay usually covers only 6 months.
  4. Decide when to start. Weigh starting at 62, at full retirement age or at 70 in when should I start Social Security?
  5. Put it on one timeline. A quick check with the Can I retire? tool shows your pension and Social Security at 62, 67 and 70.

Common questions

Does my Oregon PERS or FERS pension reduce my Social Security?

No. Since the Social Security Fairness Act, no government pension reduces Social Security. Most PERS jobs and all FERS jobs paid Social Security tax anyway, so most of these members were never affected.

Do I need to do anything to get the higher amount?

If you were already getting a reduced benefit and Social Security has your address and bank details, no. If you never applied because of WEP or GPO, you may need to apply.

Will I get back pay to 2024 if I apply now?

Not usually. The law didn't change the back-pay rule, so retirement and survivor benefits generally go back no more than 6 months before the month you apply.

Do my years in a job without Social Security now count toward my benefit?

No. Only earnings that paid Social Security tax count. The law removed the extra reduction, not the basic rule.

Can I get a widow's benefit and my teacher pension at the same time?

Yes. The offset that used to reduce widow and widower benefits is gone, so you can get both if you qualify.

Could the law be changed back?

Congress can always change Social Security law, but as of October 2026 the repeal is in effect. Check ssa.gov for current rules before you file.

General information, not advice. We aren't part of the Social Security Administration. Your statement at ssa.gov has your official estimate, and Social Security can answer questions about your record at 1-800-772-1213.
