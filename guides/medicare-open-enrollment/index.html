Medicare open enrollment: what to check before December 7

Rules checked October 31, 2026, against medicare.gov and opm.gov

Short answer

Medicare's open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7 each year. During it, anyone with Medicare can switch between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage, change Medicare Advantage plans, or join, switch or drop a Part D drug plan. Changes start January 1. Even if you like your plan, check it: plans can change their premiums, drug lists and doctor networks every year.

What you can change

Between October 15 and December 7 you can (Medicare.gov):

  • Move from Original Medicare to a Medicare Advantage plan, or back.
  • Switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to another.
  • Join a Part D drug plan, switch to a different one, or drop it.

Your plan must receive your choice by December 7. The new coverage starts January 1. If you do nothing, you usually stay in the plan you have, with whatever changes it made for next year.

This window is not for signing up for Medicare the first time. That has its own timing around your 65th birthday, explained in when to sign up for Medicare.

Five things to check this fall

  1. Your plan's letter about next year. Medicare Advantage and Part D plans send an Annual Notice of Change each fall. It lists what changes on January 1: premium, deductible, copays and covered drugs.
  2. Your drugs. Make a list of every prescription and dose. Enter it in Medicare Plan Finder to see what each plan near you would cost for the whole year, not just the premium.
  3. Your doctors and hospitals. Medicare Advantage plans use networks that can change. Call your main doctors' offices and ask which plans they will take next year.
  4. Your pharmacy. Some plans charge much less at "preferred" pharmacies.
  5. Travel and second homes. If you spend part of the year in another state, check how each plan covers care there.
Switching from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare

You can switch back during open enrollment, but think about a Medigap (Medicare Supplement) policy first. Outside your first Medigap sign-up window and a few special situations, insurers in most states can turn you down or charge more because of your health. Check your state's rules with your local SHIP office before you drop a Medicare Advantage plan.

If you have retiree health coverage

Many public retirees have health coverage from their former employer. Joining a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan on your own can end or change that coverage, so check before you act.

  • Federal retirees (FEHB): your FEHB plan keeps working alongside Medicare. FEHB has its own open season, November 9 to December 14, 2026. See FEHB open season for retirees.
  • Postal retirees (PSHB): the Postal Service Health Benefits program has its own Medicare rules, including Part B and a built-in drug plan. Read your PSHB plan brochure before joining a separate Part D plan.
  • State and local retirees: plans such as Oregon PERS's PHIP or a CalPERS Medicare plan often include drug coverage. Joining another drug plan can cancel your retiree plan. Ask your retirement system first. See Oregon PERS retiree health insurance.

If you miss December 7

  • January 1 to March 31: if you are in a Medicare Advantage plan on January 1, you can make one change: switch to another Medicare Advantage plan, or go back to Original Medicare (and join a drug plan). You cannot move from Original Medicare into Medicare Advantage in this window.
  • Special enrollment periods: moving, losing other coverage and a few other events let you change plans at other times.

What to do next

  1. Find your plan's Annual Notice of Change and read what changes January 1.
  2. Compare plans with your drug list in Medicare Plan Finder.
  3. For free, unbiased one-on-one help, call your state's SHIP program (shiphelp.org) or Medicare at 1-800-633-4227.
  4. Watch for the 2027 Part B premium, which Medicare usually announces in November. We'll add it to retirement numbers for 2027.

Common questions

Do I have to do anything during Medicare open enrollment?

No. If you do nothing, you usually keep your current plan for next year. It is still worth checking, because premiums, drug lists and networks can change on January 1.

When does a change made in open enrollment start?

January 1 of the next year, as long as the plan receives your choice by December 7.

Can I change Medigap during open enrollment?

Medicare's open enrollment doesn't cover Medigap policies. You can apply for a different Medigap policy at any time, but outside your first sign-up window the insurer may be able to use your health to decide.

I'm a federal retiree. Do I need Part D?

Most FEHB plans include drug coverage, so many federal retirees don't join a separate Part D plan. Postal retirees in PSHB have different rules. Read your plan brochure before deciding.

Where can I get free help choosing?

Your state's SHIP program gives free, unbiased Medicare counseling. Find yours at shiphelp.org.
