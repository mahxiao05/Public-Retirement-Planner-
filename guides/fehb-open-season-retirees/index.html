FEHB open season for retirees: November 9 to December 14, 2026

Rules checked October 31, 2026, against opm.gov

Short answer

The 2026 Federal Benefits Open Season runs November 9 to December 14, 2026. Federal retirees can change their FEHB or PSHB plan, switch between self only, self plus one and family, and join or change FEDVIP dental and vision. OPM says the average FEHB enrollee share of premiums rises 10.9% for 2027, and PSHB rises 8.2%, so it pays to compare.

What retirees can change

During open season, annuitants can (OPM: Open Season):

  • Switch to a different FEHB plan or option (or, for postal annuitants, a different PSHB plan).
  • Change enrollment type: self only, self plus one, or self and family.
  • Join FEDVIP dental and vision, or change or drop those plans.

Changes usually start in January 2027. If you do nothing, your current plan carries over at its 2027 rate.

Flexible spending accounts (FSAFEDS) are for current employees only, so retirees can skip that part.

What 2027 costs look like

OPM's 2027 summary gives these averages (OPM: Open Season Highlights, 2027 plan year):

ProgramAverage change in what you pay
FEHBUp 10.9%
PSHB (postal)Up 8.2%

Those are averages. Some plans rise much more and a few go down, so look up your own plan's 2027 rate rather than assuming. Retirees pay the same premium as employees, but monthly from the annuity instead of every two weeks from pay.

If you have Medicare too

FEHB keeps working after you turn 65. When you have Medicare Parts A and B, Medicare usually pays first and FEHB second. Things to compare:

  • Lower out-of-pocket costs with Part B. Many FEHB plans waive deductibles and copays for people who also have Part B, and some pay back part of the Part B premium.
  • Medicare Advantage options. Some FEHB plans offer their own Medicare Advantage version with extra benefits for annuitants.
  • Postal retirees. PSHB has its own rules on Medicare Part B and includes a Medicare drug plan for people with Medicare. Read the PSHB brochure for your situation.

Medicare's own open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7, overlapping with FEHB open season. See Medicare open enrollment.

How to make a change

  1. Compare plans with OPM's plan comparison tool and each plan's 2027 brochure on opm.gov.
  2. Make your change through OPM Retirement Services Online, or by calling OPM Retirement Services at 1-888-767-6738.
  3. Watch for a confirmation and a new ID card from your new plan before January.

Choices to be careful with

Never cancel FEHB to "save money"

If you cancel FEHB as a retiree, you generally can't get it back. Suspending it for certain other coverage (such as Medicare Advantage or TRICARE) is different and can be reversed under OPM's rules. Read keeping FEHB, PSHB and FEGLI in retirement first.

Dropping to self only can affect your spouse

If your spouse is covered by your FEHB and you die, they can keep FEHB only if they get a survivor annuity and were covered under your enrollment. Changing to self only can cut them off. See FERS survivor benefit.

Common questions

When is FEHB open season in 2026?

November 9 to December 14, 2026, for coverage in the 2027 plan year.

How much are FEHB premiums going up for 2027?

OPM says the average enrollee share rises 10.9% for FEHB and 8.2% for PSHB. Your own plan may rise more or less.

Do I have to do anything if I'm happy with my plan?

No. Your enrollment continues automatically at the new rate. Still check the 2027 brochure for benefit changes.

Can I change plans outside open season?

Only after certain life events, such as marriage, divorce, losing other coverage or moving out of a plan's area.

Who do retirees call about FEHB?

OPM Retirement Services, not your old agency. Call 1-888-767-6738 or use Retirement Services Online.

General information, not advice. We aren't part of OPM. Check your plan brochure and OPM's open season pages before you change your coverage.
