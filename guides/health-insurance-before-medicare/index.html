How do I get health insurance if I retire before 65?

Rules checked October 18, 2026, against opm.gov, oregon.gov/pers, dol.gov, healthcare.gov, medicare.gov and cms.gov

Short answer

Medicare starts at 65 no matter when you retire. If you retire earlier, you need coverage for the years in between. Federal retirees can usually keep their FEHB plan. Oregon PERS retirees can buy a PERS plan made for retirees not yet on Medicare. Other choices include COBRA, a spouse's plan, or a HealthCare.gov plan, which may come with help paying the premium.

Federal retirees: keeping FEHB

To carry your FEHB plan into retirement, you must retire on an immediate annuity (MRA+10 counts) and have been enrolled, or covered as a family member, for the 5 years of service just before your annuity starts. If you've had fewer than 5 years of chances to enroll, coverage since your first chance counts. Retirees get the same government share of the premium as employees, and their share is taken out of the annuity (OPM: annuitants).

  • Postponed MRA+10. If you leave and postpone your annuity, FEHB stops when you leave. You can bridge the gap with Temporary Continuation of Coverage (TCC) and re-enroll in FEHB when the annuity starts.
  • Deferred retirement. If you leave before you're eligible for an immediate annuity, you can't re-enroll in FEHB later (OPM: types of retirement).
  • TCC. Federal workers who leave and can't keep FEHB can buy TCC for up to 18 months at the full premium plus 2%. You generally have 60 days after the notice or after leaving, whichever is later, to sign up. Retirees who keep regular FEHB can't use TCC (OPM: TCC).
  • Your spouse after you die. A surviving spouse can keep FEHB only if a survivor annuity is paid to them and they were covered under your Self Plus One or Self and Family plan (OPM: survivor benefits). See the FERS survivor benefit and FEHB and FEGLI in retirement.

Oregon PERS retirees: PHIP and subsidies

The PERS Health Insurance Program (PHIP) is open to PERS retirees, their spouses and eligible dependents who live in a plan's service area in the U.S. For people not yet on Medicare it offers Kaiser Permanente plans (an HMO and a high-deductible plan) and UnitedHealthcare plans (a PPO and a high-deductible plan), plus dental plans from Kaiser and Delta Dental. Once you're eligible for Medicare, PHIP requires you to have both Part A and Part B. PHIP customer service is 800-768-7377 (PERS: PHIP).

Some Tier One and Tier Two retirees get help with the cost. RHIPA helps eligible state retirees who aren't yet on Medicare, with the amount based on years of state service. RHIA pays $60 a month toward a PHIP Medicare plan. OPSRP retirees don't get either subsidy (PERS: understanding retiree health insurance). PERS suggests comparing PHIP with your employer's own retiree options. See PERS retiree health insurance.

Example: pricing a five-year gap

Example

Terry is a made-up school employee in Oregon PERS who plans to retire at 60, five years before Medicare. Terry's spouse is 58. To compare choices, Terry lists for each one:

  1. Monthly premium for both of them, from the PHIP rate sheet, the district's retiree plan, COBRA's quote and a HealthCare.gov preview.
  2. The deductible and out-of-pocket maximum, since a bad year costs the maximum, not the premium.
  3. How long it lasts. COBRA ends after 18 months, so it can cover only part of a five-year gap. PHIP or a HealthCare.gov plan can run until 65.
  4. The spouse's gap. When Terry turns 65 and moves to Medicare, the spouse is 63 and still needs coverage for two more years.

Terry then multiplies each plan's yearly cost by the years it would be used. A HealthCare.gov plan's price depends on household income, so Terry runs the preview using expected pension income, not current pay.

How to choose

  • Price the whole year, not just the premium. Add up the premium, the deductible and what you'd pay for your usual doctors and prescriptions.
  • Check your doctors and drugs. Make sure the plan's network includes them before you switch.
  • Avoid a gap. Line up the new plan so it starts the day after your job coverage ends.
  • Plan for your spouse too. If your spouse is younger, they may need coverage after you've moved to Medicare.
  • Mind your income. Help with HealthCare.gov premiums depends on household income, which you can partly control in early retirement by choosing when to draw on savings. The rules for that help have changed several times, so check HealthCare.gov's current estimate rather than an old article.

When you turn 65

Sign up for Medicare during your 7-month window around your 65th birthday. The window starts 3 months before the month you turn 65 and ends 3 months after it. If you sign up before your birthday month, Part B starts the month you turn 65. COBRA and retiree plans don't let you put off Part B without a penalty. Only coverage from a current job does, and then you have 8 months after that job or coverage ends to sign up. Signing up late adds 10% to your Part B premium for each full year you could have signed up, for as long as you have it (Medicare: when coverage starts and avoiding penalties).

The standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month in 2026, with a $283 deductible. If your income two years earlier was above $109,000 (single) or $218,000 (joint), you pay more, a surcharge called IRMAA (CMS: 2026 Medicare premiums). See when to sign up for Medicare and Medicare IRMAA.

FEHB and PHIP both work alongside Medicare after 65, so ask your plan how the two fit together before you decide on Part B.

What to do next

  1. Ask your benefits office first. Find out whether your employer offers a retiree plan, and whether you meet FEHB's 5-year rule if you're federal.
  2. Get real quotes. Price PHIP, COBRA or TCC, and a HealthCare.gov plan for the exact months you need.
  3. Set the start date. Pick a plan that starts the day after job coverage ends, and note any 60-day deadline.
  4. Mark your Medicare window. Put the 3 months before your 65th birthday month on your calendar.
  5. Check the whole plan. The quick Can I retire? tool and the planner show how premiums fit with your pension.

Common questions

Can I keep my federal health insurance if I retire at 57?

Yes, if you retire on an immediate annuity and had FEHB for the 5 years before it starts. If you postpone an MRA+10 annuity, coverage pauses until the annuity begins.

Can PERS retirees under 65 get PERS health insurance?

Yes. PHIP has plans for retirees not yet on Medicare from Kaiser Permanente and UnitedHealthcare.

How long does COBRA last?

Usually up to 18 months after you leave your job, and you may pay the full cost plus 2%.

Does COBRA count as job coverage for delaying Medicare?

No. Medicare says COBRA isn't group health coverage from current work, so it doesn't protect you from the Part B penalty.

Can I buy a HealthCare.gov plan when I retire?

Yes. Losing job coverage lets you sign up from 60 days before it ends to 60 days after.

What if my spouse is younger than me?

Your spouse needs their own coverage until they turn 65. Under FEHB or PHIP, they can usually stay on your plan.

General information, not advice. Plans, prices and rules change every year. Check with your plan, your benefits office or HealthCare.gov before you decide.
